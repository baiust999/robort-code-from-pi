## II. RELATED WORK

This section places the system against the four challenges of Section I-A. The search was not systematic, and no PRISMA flow is claimed. In October 2026 we queried OpenAlex for records from 2022 onward on low-cost and Raspberry Pi rescue robots, rescue-robot victim communication, WebRTC teleoperation, link-loss failsafes, watchdog timers and software fault recovery in mobile robots, and selected candidates from the relevance-ranked results by title and abstract and by backward citation from the reviews below. Table I includes peer-reviewed platforms from 2022 onward that present a built or simulated SAR robot and whose full text was read, with one earlier exception noted below.

### A. Reviews and Levels of Operator Involvement

Reviews organize SAR robotics along different axes: a domain theory of robot-assisted search [@murphy2004hri], the control perspective [@liu2013], multi-robot coordination and perception [@queralta2020], and readiness for deployment, where Delmerico et al. observe that field-deployable rescue technology has requirements for speed, versatility and ease of use that current systems may not meet [@delmerico2019]. Schneider and Wildermuth set out SAR requirements for each component of a robot [@schneider2016]. More recent reviews organize the field by system architecture, from 74 papers of 2016-2024 [@kareem2026]; by platform class, from more than 190 studies of unmanned ground vehicles (UGVs) [@sinha2026]; by perception deficiencies [@tong2024]; by ethical and design issues [@chitikena2023]; and, for mines, by design requirements [@bakzadeh2026]. The open problems they name include multi-robot coordination, real-time decision-making, energy and standardized benchmarks [@kareem2026; @sinha2026]. Neither the timing of the stop path nor the on-board cost of victim interaction is an axis along which they organize the field.

Levels of autonomy range from teleoperation to full autonomy [@beer2014], and SAR robots are in practice teleoperated or semi-autonomous [@schneider2016]. The system presented here is a teleoperation system, because rescue robots are typically teleoperated in actual disasters [@daun2023]. In the Urban Circuit of the DARPA Subterranean Challenge, where communication constraints make underground search harder, one team used teleoperation as the baseline against which autonomous solutions were compared [@isaacs2022].

### B. Field Deployments, Benchmarks and Recent Platforms

The World Trade Center response in 2001 is the first known use of robots for urban search and rescue [@murphy2004]; its post-hoc analysis called for perceptual and assistive interfaces and formal models of the robot's state [@casper2003]. At Fukushima Daiichi in 2011 the robot was fitted with a wired link because wireless communication was not expected to work inside the reactor buildings [@nagatani2013], and organization, operation scheme and systemization were the major lessons [@kawatsuma2012]. After the 2016 Amatrice earthquake, ground and aerial robots collected data for 3D models of damaged churches [@kruijff2016]. We therefore treat link loss as an expected operating condition rather than a rare fault. Standard test methods for response robots have been refined through competitions [@jacoff2012], and the DARPA Subterranean Challenge evaluated integrated systems underground [@chung2023]. Section V does not use standardized apparatus, so its results are not comparable with benchmark scores.

The recent platforms in Table I concentrate on mobility, detection or communication range rather than on the control path. They range from a tracked robot with flippers and a manipulator scored at the World Robot Summit 2020 [@megalingam2022] to commodity designs: TXRob streams camera, microphone and gas data from a Raspberry Pi 4 over local Wi-Fi [@verano2022]; Pramono et al. combine ESP32 controllers, Bluetooth control and Internet-connected video [@pramono2024]; and a Raspberry Pi 4B quadruped reports a 200 m control range over Wi-Fi [@deshmukh2025]. Others extend range with a LoRa network that needs no infrastructure [@manuel2022], use a LoRa command link with Wi-Fi video and a cloud dashboard [@gehlot2025], add voice control and person tracking through a cloud-connected controller [@cafolla2026], or change the mobility class [@jia2025; @yamauchi2022]. Detection-oriented work locates survivors with a thermal infrared array [@kim2023], a YOLOv8 detector on a Jetson Nano [@raghavan2026], multimodal fusion validated in simulation [@khattab2025], or Bluetooth Low Energy wearables [@cantizani2022]. Five of these designs are described as low-cost, affordable, cost-effective or built from inexpensive parts [@verano2022; @pramono2024; @kim2023; @raghavan2026; @deshmukh2025], yet none states a numeric build cost. The earlier Survivor Buddy head does, at USD 3,600 for version 1.0 and USD 1,800 for version 2.0 [@murphy2011].

### C. Fail-Safe and Fault-Tolerant Architectures

IEC 60204-1 defines stop categories for machinery, of which category 0 is stopping by immediate removal of power to the actuators [@iec60204]. Industrial practice is moving safety functions from relays and interlock circuits into software [@murray2022], which makes the placement of the stop decision a design choice. Pont presents patterns for time-triggered embedded systems whose task timing can be predicted [@pont2001]; the firmware of Section III-E follows that style. Watchdog timers detect a stalled controller: a hardware watchdog designed for drone processors resets the processor when it is not serviced in time [@madhushankara2024]. That mechanism detects a hung processor, not the absence of operator commands. For loss of the operator link, the ArduPilot Rover documentation describes a radio failsafe with a configurable timeout (default 1 s) and a ground-station heartbeat failsafe (default 5 s) [@ardupilot_rover_failsafe]; these are configurable timeouts in general-purpose vehicle software, not measured stop times on a rescue robot. A run-time monitor for networked robots under delayed observations [@narasimhan2024] addresses late information, which is complementary to a stop on missing commands.

For process supervision, Candea et al. argue that systems built to recover fast may be more productive than systems built never to fail [@candea2004]. Schrick et al. use microservices and a hierarchical state machine to increase the interruptibility of a delivery robot [@schrick2025], Ramesh et al. estimate robot health at run time so that a supervisor can intervene [@ramesh2022], and failure mode and effects analysis combined with simulation has been used to assess how rover software mitigates sensor and actuator hardware failures [@sini2022]. None of these targets a single-board rescue robot, and no platform in Table I reports a firmware-level stop or supervised process recovery.

### D. Human-Robot Interaction (HRI): Operator and Victim

On the operator side, situation awareness [@endsley1995] is the recurring concern. Limited video bandwidth, time lags and low frame rates degrade teleoperation performance [@chen2007], network latency affects operators' perception and decisions in vehicle teleoperation [@kamtam2024], and even small latencies can raise workload in planetary exploration [@luz2023]. For mine rescue, interface principles have been mapped from cognitive load, situational awareness and trust [@bakzadeh2025], and rescue personnel report a need for real-time situational data [@bakzadeh2026b]. Semantic indicators can signal when a scene needs the operator's attention [@ruan2025], and LED-based affective expressions improved participants' situational awareness in an online study [@akgun2023].

On the victim side, Bethel and Murphy applied non-verbal affective expression to victim assessment within about three metres [@bethel2007]. Survivor Buddy, a four-degree-of-freedom head with a touch-screen face, was built to study robots as a social medium between trapped survivors and the outside world [@murphy2010]; its second version was 50% less expensive and 78% lighter [@murphy2011], and its reported evaluations are a quasi-experimental study that the authors describe as neither statistically significant nor rigorously controlled [@murphy2010] and two simulated scenario studies [@srinivasan2012]. Among recent low-cost platforms the trapped person is mainly an object of detection [@raghavan2026; @khattab2025; @kim2023; @cantizani2022; @deshmukh2025]; one cloud-connected design analyses the robot's video and audio to detect survivors [@sharma2022]. Where speech toward the person is mentioned, it is described rather than tested [@cafolla2026; @yamauchi2022]. TXRob's microphone relays sound to the operator only [@verano2022], SERR provisions a speaker and microphone that are not yet implemented [@khattab2025], and Rescuer gives the operator a push-to-talk walkie-talkie while its camera video is recorded on the robot rather than streamed [@manuel2022].

### E. Real-Time Media and Computing on Edge Hardware

WebRTC has been used for teleoperation with strict latency targets: a remote dental-surgery robot on Amazon Kinesis Video Streams WebRTC reports end-to-end latency below 50 ms [@shi2025webrtc], and a multi-operator platform for a mobile manipulator uses dedicated web and signalling servers and public STUN servers [@tiberkak2023]. Both depend on servers outside the robot's network; the robot described here runs both WebRTC endpoints and signalling on the local network. Opus supports frame sizes from 2.5 ms to 60 ms, with 20 ms described as a good choice for most applications [@valin2012], and Bachhuber and Steinbach present a glass-to-glass delay measurement system [@bachhuber2016]. The cloud-to-edge continuum has been proposed for the low-delay requirements of SAR, though building it remains an open challenge [@militano2023], and several recent platforms send alerts or streams through the cloud [@raghavan2026; @cafolla2026], presuming infrastructure that a collapsed site may not provide.

The gap that Table I shows is a combination rather than a missing feature, and it rests on what each paper reports, which is weaker than what each robot lacks. Stop categories are standardized [@iec60204] and recovery by restart is established [@candea2004], yet no recent platform in the table reports a stop time bounded independently of its higher-level software (C1) or supervised recovery of its processes (C2). Two-way communication with the trapped person is a stated requirement [@schneider2016], a feature of fielded mine-rescue robots [@bakzadeh2026] and the subject of an HRI literature [@bethel2007; @murphy2010; @srinivasan2012], yet among recent low-cost platforms the victim is mainly a detection target and none reports two-way audio and video with the victim on single-board hardware or its resource cost (C3). Low cost is asserted without a figure (C4). Section III brings these established mechanisms together on commodity hardware, and Section V reports which properties the measurements support and which they do not yet cover.
